# Vision

Pumblechook is a one-page portfolio painted like a picture-book collage: a deep-sea diver's dream with one central sea creature, and a project on each of five arms. A visitor reads a short logline for each project and follows a link to its live site or its public repository. The owner's name, a one-line bio, and GitHub and LinkedIn profile links live inside the picture too.

**For:** someone weighing the owner's software and data work (a hiring manager, a client, a peer) who arrives from a résumé or profile link with a minute to spare.

**MVP success:** a visitor opens pumblechook.com on a desktop or a phone, sees the scene, finds all five projects, reads each logline, and opens each live site and each public repository. A visitor to pumblechook.dev lands on pumblechook.com.

**Principles:**

- The page is a picture. No boxes, grids, or navigation bars.
- Every element is part of the active art. Nothing sits in a header, a footer, or a title block; the name, the bio, and every link are objects in the scene.
- No flat colour anywhere. Every surface carries a texture and a gradient.
- Uneven by design: hand-cut edges, off-kilter placement, nothing aligned to a grid.
- The scene is slowly alive: eyes blink, light wavers, an arm wriggles under the pointer.
- Two compositions of one scene: a wide one for desktops and a tall one for phones, each arranged on purpose.
- Links never hide. Every project is reachable by touch, by keyboard, by screen reader, and with motion turned off.

**Not in the MVP:** project detail pages or case studies, a blog, a content system, accounts, a contact form, and sound.
